# The metronome and practice tools

## A metronome that knows the meter

An empty groove is a free metronome: a click in fifteen time signatures with the one accented, a count-in, tap tempo, straight or swung, and a tempo that climbs or winds down by itself every few bars.

Write notes into it and the click becomes a groove. Mute the snare and play it yourself while the kit keeps time.

## Practice properly

- **Loop** a couple of bars.
- **Drop out:** have the groove drop out for a bar or two, and find out whether you're still with it when it comes back.
- **Feel:** set the feel from straight to deep in the pocket, and hear a laid-back backbeat before you try to play one.
- **Count:** a counter tells you which bar you're on and how long you've played.

## Learn it

In the **Practice** sheet, one chip starts a groove slower and climbs back to its own tempo, five at a time every four bars, count-in included. It stops when it gets there.

## Work a rudiment round

Write a paradiddle, then have Tating turn it over every 2, 4 or 8 bars while you keep playing: every inversion in one pass, without a hand leaving the sticks. Displace the whole groove instead and the backbeat moves against the barline.

## Fills, heard where they go

Press and hold **Fill** for a fill every 2, 4 or 8 bars. The last bar of each phrase plays a made-up fill and the grid shows it as it sounds; press the lit chip to stop. Nothing is written to your groove.

A single press of **Fill** still makes one up and writes it into the groove.

## Write the song down

Gig charts: a song as its form (intro, verse, chorus, the repeats and the road map) for a music stand, beside your grooves.
